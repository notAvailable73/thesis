0.  PAPER-GROUNDED REASONING (per thesis_implementation_instructions Section 5)
================================================================================
Step 10 introduces no new method -- every component in the grid (Bottleneck /
LoRA adapters, evidential / softmax prototype heads, ResNet-18 /
MobileNetV3-Small backbones, CIFAR-FS / MiniImageNet datasets) was already
paper-grounded and justified in Steps 4-9's own writeups (step_writeups/
step4_5.txt Section 0, step5.txt, step8.txt, step9.txt Section 0). Re-arguing
those choices here would be re-litigating settled decisions rather than
adding anything -- see progress.txt's "Known state of the science" note and
this repo's CLAUDE.md instruction not to re-litigate without new evidence.

Two things ARE new in Step 10's own scope, and both are protocol questions,
not method questions:

  - 1-shot (k_shot=1). Snell et al. 2017 ("Prototypical Networks for
    Few-shot Learning") defines the prototype as the mean of the support
    embeddings per class; at k=1 that mean degenerates to the single support
    embedding itself, which the paper's own formulation already covers (it
    is not a special case requiring new code) -- src/heads/prototype_head.py
    computes the class mean generically over the k axis. What is genuinely
    untested is not the formula but the TRAINED RECIPE: the KL-anneal
    schedule and evidence affine (scale 2 / bias -6) were tuned only on
    5-shot episodes (Step 4.5's VAL sweep), and a k=1 support set gives the
    evidential loss far less signal per episode. This is a recipe-transfer
    risk, not a paper-fidelity risk, and is exactly what plan.md Section 2's
    pre-flight smoke test checks before committing GPU-hours to it.
  - LoRA x MobileNetV3-Small / LoRA x MiniImageNet. Hu et al. 2021's LoRA
    formulation (W_eff = W_frozen + (alpha/rank) A@B) is architecture-
    agnostic by construction -- src/adapters/lora.py's
    default_lora_targets() already auto-detects the MobileNetV3 family (Step
    8) and applies the same reparameterization to whichever 1x1 conv is
    named. Nothing about the paper's method changes per backbone or
    dataset; what is untested is purely PLUMBING (has anyone actually run
    this combination end-to-end and confirmed it does not collapse), which
    is again the smoke test's job, not a new derivation.

PROS / CONS / FIT
  PROS: reusing Steps 4-9's already-justified components means Step 10 can
    be a pure controlled comparison -- every one of the 120 cells differs
    from every other only along the grid's own 5 axes (dataset, shots,
    backbone, adapter, head) plus seed, with the training recipe itself held
    FROZEN (plan.md Section 3's explicit decision). That is what makes the
    resulting table a fair head-to-head instead of 120 independently-tuned
    numbers.
  CONS: "frozen recipe" is a real limitation, not a free lunch -- the recipe
    was VAL-tuned once, on ResNet-18 x CIFAR-FS x 5-shot (Step 4.5), and
    carried unchanged across 2 backbones x 2 datasets x 2 shot regimes it
    was never re-tuned for. Steps 8 and 9 already flagged the
    backbone/dataset generalization gap this produces; Step 10 adds a third
    untuned axis (1-shot) on top. Section 5 below carries this caveat
    forward explicitly, and Section 10.9 (a bounded VAL-only side-study,
    not yet run) is the planned mitigation -- not folded into the main grid,
    so it cannot silently contaminate the headline numbers.
  FIT: this is exactly the "Minimum Viable Thesis" grid proposal.txt
    Section 6 and implementation.txt Section 10 both specify as mandatory;
    nothing here is optional scope.

DEVIATION / LIMITATION CARRIED INTO THE CODE
  - The metrics-JSON schema is frozen (instructions.txt), and the results
    filename convention does not encode dataset/shots/backbone/seed --
    exactly as step9.txt Section 0 already noted for the dataset axis, this
    is not a paper-driven constraint but a pre-existing engineering
    invariant this step had to design around (configs/grid/_index.json is
    the sidecar; see scripts/build_grid_configs.py's module docstring).
  - The "re-render at 300 dpi" plots (scripts/grid_plots.py) are a
    mechanical raster upscale of the EXISTING 200-dpi PNGs
    scripts/evaluate.py already writes, not a fresh vector regeneration --
    evaluate.py does not persist the raw pooled probabilities anywhere a
    downstream script could re-plot from, and this step deliberately does
    not modify evaluate.py (a previous step's frozen entry point) to add
    that. Flagged honestly rather than silently claiming higher real
    resolution than the source data supports.

  4.1  ACCURACY AND MACRO-F1 (results/mvt_table_accuracy.{tex,png})
       Best cell per (dataset, shots), accuracy % / macro-F1 %:
         CIFAR-FS      1-shot   81.14 / 80.02   r18 full_ft softmax

       Macro-F1 tracks accuracy but sits consistently 0.5-1.5 pp BELOW it,
       and the gap widens at 1-shot. Mechanically this is what macro-F1 is
       for: at k=1 a single badly-placed prototype collapses recall on one
       class only, which macro-averaging punishes and accuracy partly
       absorbs. F1 was reported for every cell (f1_macro_mean/std/ci95) and
       never reorders a comparison in this grid -- no conclusion below
       changes if F1 is substituted for accuracy.

       Seed stability: across-seed std of the mean accuracy exceeds 1 pp in
       only 2 of 40 configurations (both CIFAR-FS x r18 x LoRA x evidential:
       1.09 pp at 1-shot, 1.07 pp at 5-shot); every other cell is <= 0.74 pp.

       NOT-A-BUG finding worth recording: full_ft and linear_probe return
       BIT-IDENTICAL metrics for seeds 42/43/44, down to the early-stopping
       epoch. Cause: the grid configs vary only `seed:`, and cfg.seed reaches
       training solely via set_seed() i.e. via random parameter init -- the
       training episode stream comes from cfg.trainer.train_seed_offset, a
       fixed config value, independent of cfg.seed (scripts/train.py:270,
       :287). Full-FT initialises from pretrained ImageNet weights and
       linear-probe has no parameters, so neither has a randomly-initialised
       tensor for the seed to perturb; Bottleneck and LoRA do, which is
       exactly why only those show spread. Consequence to carry into any
       write-up: for the two BASELINE rows, n=3 seeds is effectively n=1 and
       there is no variance estimate.

  4.6  RQ-BY-RQ VERDICTS
       RQ1 (adapter placement / parameter-accuracy tradeoff) -- ANSWERED.
         The parallel bottleneck beats LoRA in 16/16 matched comparisons
         (same dataset, shots, backbone, head), by +2.13 to +8.26 pp. No
         configuration exists in this grid where LoRA wins. Two distinct
         regimes inside that result:
           - On MobileNetV3-Small it is a STRICT PARETO WIN: parallel is both
             cheaper (6,930 vs 10,754 params) and better (+2.13 to +4.85 pp).
             There is no tradeoff to negotiate; LoRA is dominated.
           - On ResNet-18 there IS a tradeoff: parallel costs 2.6x the params
             (31,746 vs 12,290) and buys +3.12 to +8.26 pp.
         Against the mandatory baselines (CIFAR-FS, r18): at 5-shot the
         31,744-param parallel adapter beats 11,176,512-param full
         fine-tuning by +0.98 pp (91.44 vs 90.47) on 0.28% of the trainable
         budget, and MobileNetV3-Small's 6,928-param adapter MATCHES it
         (90.74 +/- 0.17 vs 90.47) on 0.06%. At 1-shot the ordering flips and
         full-FT wins by 2.57 pp -- the honest limit of the claim.
         Interpretation (not re-litigating settled ground, just naming it):
         LoRA is a transformer-native reparameterisation of dense weight
         matrices; on convolutional backbones a conv-shaped parallel adapter
         is the better-matched inductive bias, and the grid says so
         consistently across 2 datasets x 2 shots x 2 backbones x 2 heads.

       RQ2 (does evidential calibrate better than softmax under a tiny
       trainable budget) -- ANSWERED, NEGATIVE, 0/20.
         No. Section 4.2: worse than plain softmax in every one of 20 matched
         pairs and worse than TS-softmax by up to 51x, with no accuracy
         compensation. This is not a configuration accident: it holds across
         2 datasets x 2 shot regimes x 2 backbones x 4 adapters, including
         the 2-trainable-parameter linear-probe cell -- i.e. it holds
         everywhere the proposal's "<500 trainable params" condition is met
         and everywhere it is not. The grid converts Step 4.5's single-config
         finding (~7x, one configuration) into a well-powered negative
         result. Treat RQ2 as settled in the negative.

       RQ3 (does the Bayesian prior improve near-OOD detection in low-data
       regimes) -- ANSWERED, SPLIT VERDICT.
         Against probabilistic softmax scores: YES, decisively and with the
         predicted low-data trend -- 37/40 near-OOD wins over MSP (mean
         +0.053 AUROC) and 38/40 over TS-MSP (+0.067), with the advantage
         LARGER at 1-shot than 5-shot (Section 4.3).
         Against the training-free energy score: NO -- vacuity wins only
         10/40 far and 14/40 near, mean dAUROC -0.022 / -0.007.
         *** THIS IS A CHANGE TO THE RECORDED STATE OF THE SCIENCE. ***
         CLAUDE.md and step_writeups/step4_5.txt record the Tier-3 verdict
         that evidential beats energy on far-OOD and CIFAR-100-near and loses
         only on TinyImageNet-near. That was one configuration. Across 40
         configurations energy is the better OOD score in ~70% of
         comparisons, including on far-OOD where Step 4.5 had evidential
         ahead. The Step 4.5 result is not contradicted on its own cell --
         it simply does not generalise, and the grid is the stronger
         evidence. The defensible claim is now narrower: among scores derived
         from the model's own PREDICTIVE DISTRIBUTION, Dirichlet vacuity is a
         substantially better OOD ranker than MSP with or without temperature
         scaling, and its margin grows as shots shrink -- but it does not
         beat a well-chosen logit-space score. Anyone writing the thesis
         chapter should state it that way and not the broader way.

       RQ4 (latency vs uncertainty-quality Pareto frontier) -- NOT THIS STEP.

5.  HONEST CAVEATS
================================================================================
  - THE CENTRAL CAVEAT ON EVERY ACCURACY NUMBER: both backbones are
    ImageNet-pretrained and frozen, while the standard CIFAR-FS /
    MiniImageNet protocol trains the backbone from scratch on the 64 base
    classes. On MiniImageNet this is acute -- its classes ARE ImageNet
    classes, so every "novel" test class was seen in pretraining, which is
    why 95.88% at 5-shot is not a few-shot result in the sense the benchmark
    was designed to measure. Inherited from step_writeups/step9.txt Section
    5, unchanged and un-mitigated by this step. Consequence, stated plainly:
    this grid's accuracies must NOT be tabulated against from-scratch
    few-shot SOTA as if they competed. docs/RESULTS_MASTER.md Section 4
    splits the comparison into the two regimes and cites the P>M>F authors'
    own decision to relegate their supervised-ImageNet row to supplemental
    material for exactly this reason.
  - The grid recipe is FROZEN (plan.md Section 3's explicit decision, not an
    oversight): LR 5e-3, rank 16, kl_weight_max 0.1, evidence affine
    scale 2/bias -6, unchanged across all 120 cells. This means the grid
    answers "how do these axes compare under one fixed recipe", not "what
    is the best achievable number per cell" -- per-cell VAL tuning is
    explicitly out of scope for the main table (Section 10.9 side-study,
    still not run -- Section 6).
  - Two carry-overs from Steps 8/9 were inherited rather than re-examined by
    this step's CODE, but the run's own numbers now speak to both:
      (i) the mbnet/parallel/evidential checkpoint-picked-during-KL-warmup
          pattern (2-for-2 across CIFAR-FS and MiniImageNet per
          step_writeups/step9.txt Section 5) did NOT reproduce in the grid.
          kl_anneal_steps=200 at 100 episodes/epoch and one optimizer step
          per episode (src/trainers/episodic_trainer.py:237) means the KL
          weight reaches its maximum at the end of epoch 2; the grid's
          mbnet/parallel/evidential cells select best_val_epoch 3 (CIFAR-FS
          5-shot, all seeds), 5/12/5 (CIFAR-FS 1-shot), and 6 (both
          MiniImageNet buckets) -- i.e. at or after full KL weight in every
          cell. This weakens, though it does not by itself refute, the
          hypothesis that the pattern was a warm-up artefact; it is now
          0-for-4 in the grid against 2-for-2 previously, and 10.9 would
          settle it.
      (ii) the MobileNetV3-vs-ResNet-18 gap DOES grow under 1-shot, and only
          on MiniImageNet. CIFAR-FS stays flat (+0.31 / -0.23 pp at 1-shot,
          +1.34 / +0.70 at 5-shot), while MiniImageNet widens from +5.24 /
          +5.46 pp at 5-shot to +9.19 / +10.12 pp at 1-shot. Read alongside
          the pretraining-overlap caveat above, the natural reading is that
          ResNet-18's larger ImageNet representation carries more of the
          MiniImageNet answer when there is only one support image to learn
          from -- but that is an interpretation, not something this grid
          isolates, and it would need the from-scratch control to test.
  - "16 plots" is read here as dataset(2) x shots(2) x head(2) x plot-type(2)
    = 16, collapsing backbone+adapter to one data-driven "best" pick per
    (dataset, shot, head) -- the exit-criterion phrase "reliability + OOD
    histogram per dataset x head" alone would only be 4 combinations. Flagged
    per plan.md Section 6's own instruction to not let this read as a
    silently changed criterion.
  - scripts/grid_plots.py's "300 dpi" plots are a mechanical raster re-render
    of scripts/evaluate.py's existing 200-dpi PNGs (upscaled), not a fresh
    vector regeneration from raw pooled probabilities -- see Section 0.
  - Three seeds per cell, and effectively ONE for the two baselines
    (Section 4.1's not-a-bug finding). Any claim resting on a sub-1 pp margin
    over full_ft or linear_probe has no variance estimate behind it on the
    baseline side -- see the phrasing guidance in docs/RESULTS_MASTER.md
    Section 4.7 claim 2.
  - Evidential calibration is not temperature-scalable in this codebase, so
    the ECE-after-TS column exists only for softmax cells. The comparison in
    Section 4.2 is therefore structurally favourable to softmax. It is still
    the right comparison to report -- temperature scaling is cheap, standard,
    and what a practitioner would actually do -- but the asymmetry should be
    stated whenever the 51x figure is quoted.
  - Inherited unresolved issue: the TinyImageNet OOD-set class-exclusion bug
    (step_writeups/step9.txt Section 5) was traced by code inspection to be a
    test-isolation problem rather than a defect in the filtering logic, but
    was never confirmed by a logged exclusion count from a real run -- and
    this step did not add that logging either. Every TIN-near number in
    Section 4.3 carries that caveat.
  - The pre-flight smoke test (plan.md Section 2 / notebook 10a Section 4)
    covered the three genuinely-new risks (1-shot, LoRA x MobileNetV3-Small,
    LoRA x MiniImageNet); Section 4.7 records that the full run cleared all
    three. What the run does NOT tell us is whether the smoke test itself
    would have caught a collapse, since nothing collapsed.
  - Neither scripts/train.py nor scripts/evaluate.py were modified by this
    step (per explicit instruction: prior steps' notebooks must stay
    reproducible). Every naming convention this step's scripts rely on is
    imported from scripts.train, never re-derived independently. The Section
    4.5 reproducibility pass is downstream evidence that this held: the
    grid's seed-42 cells reproduce Step 6's committed metrics exactly.

================================================================================
8.  THE "CNN BACKBONES ARE OUTDATED" OBJECTION (pre-defence, 2026-08-06)
================================================================================
Raised at the pre-defence: that the thesis works on backdated technology
because nobody uses CNN backbones in 2025-26. Full answer with verified
sources: docs/DEFENCE_BRIEF.md. Recorded here because the answer is built out
of Step 10's own numbers, and because it changed this step's priority list.

  8.1  WHAT THE OBJECTION GETS RIGHT. Transformers are the strongest vision
       backbones at scale; the thesis does not dispute this and should not
       try to. DINOv3 (arXiv:2508.10104, 2025) trains a ~7B-parameter ViT on
       ~1.7B images.

  8.2  WHERE IT STOPS BEING TRUE -- three findings from the 2025 literature,
       each fetched and read this session, not recalled:
       - A memory-optimised transformer attention block still costs ~180 ms
         per inference on an STM32F746 against ~8-12 ms for CNN inference;
         MCU-class targets run 32-512 kB SRAM, <~1 MB flash, 20-200 MHz,
         often no FPU (arXiv:2506.18927, TinyML->TinyDL survey, 2025). An
         86M-parameter ViT-B/16 does not fit in 1 MB of flash under any
         quantisation scheme.
       - "In small data scenarios, the inductive bias and smaller capacity of
         CNNs improve their performance, allowing them to match that of a
         ViT... CNNs achieve comparable performance in low-data regimes even
         when the ViTs were pretrained on large-scaled datasets"
         (arXiv:2510.04794, Oct 2025). Few-shot IS the low-data regime, so
         this is a direct endorsement of the thesis's backbone choice in the
         thesis's own setting.
       - CNNs "remain the preferred choice" for edge/real-time deployment and
         deployed systems (YOLOv5/v8, Faster R-CNN, Mask R-CNN) "continue to
         rely entirely on convolutional architectures" (Scientific Reports,
         27 Nov 2025, DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-27856-3).

  8.3  THE PARAMETER/ACCURACY TRADE ON THE SAME PROTOCOL. P>M>F
       (arXiv:2204.07305 Table 4) runs 5-way few-shot episodes on CIFAR-FS
       and MiniImageNet -- our exact task -- so its accuracies and ours
       belong in one table. ViT-S/16 = 21M, ViT-B/16 = 86M (DINO model
       cards). Our numbers are the softmax parallel-bottleneck cells.
                                    CIFAR-FS      MiniImageNet
         method            train'able  1sh   5sh    1sh    5sh
         Sup-21k>PN ViT-B   ~85.8M   92.3  96.7   97.2   99.2
         CLIP>PN ViT-B      ~86.5M   85.3  93.2   93.1   98.1
         DINO>PN ViT-B      ~85.8M   84.3  92.2   95.3   98.4

       findings and must not be blurred:
         (a) At 5-shot on CIFAR-FS the saving is nearly free -- our 31,744
             params are 1.06 pp behind a fully meta-trained DINO ViT-S at
             662x fewer trainable params, and 0.76 pp behind ViT-B at
             2,703x fewer. Strongest trade in the thesis.
         (b) BACKBONE-MATCHED WE WIN OUTRIGHT: vs DINO>PN ResNet-50 we are
             +3.56 pp (mini 5-shot) and +5.83 pp (mini 1-shot) AHEAD at 788x
             fewer trainable params. The residual gap to the ViT rows is a
             ViT gap, not a parameter-efficiency gap -- this is the single
             most useful sentence for the defence.
         (c) The trade IS bad at 1-shot and on MiniImageNet with the small
             backbone: mbnet is -7.90 pp (mini 5-shot) and -18.18 pp (mini
             1-shot) vs ViT-S. Volunteer this. The 6,928-param cell is
             competitive on CIFAR-FS (-1.76 pp at 3,031x fewer) and is not
             on MiniImageNet -- consistent with Section 5's carry-over (ii)
             and the pretraining-overlap caveat.

  8.4  THE QUESTION IS CURRENT, NOT DATED. "Does uncertainty quantification
       survive parameter-efficient adaptation, and stay calibrated" produced
       Laplace-LoRA (ICLR 2024), BLoB (NeurIPS 2024), LoRA-Ensemble,
       Scalable Bayesian LoRA (2025), and in 2026 alone Stiefel-Bayes
       Adapters, BaRA, Bayesian Sparse LoRA, and a dedicated BENCHMARK
       (Bayesian Adaptation Gym). Every one except LoRA-Ensemble is on
       language models; none is few-shot episodic vision on an
       edge-deployable backbone. Fields do not build benchmarks for dead
       questions.

  8.5  TWO SURVEYS, ONE BLIND SPOT -- the strongest single gap evidence, and
       both are documented absences rather than inferences:
       - arXiv:2402.02242 (PEFT for pre-trained vision models, a survey of
         the whole ViT PEFT field) does not discuss calibration, uncertainty
         quantification, or OOD detection anywhere.
       - arXiv:2506.18927 (TinyML->TinyDL survey, 2025) covers quantisation
         extensively and makes no mention of uncertainty, calibration, or
         OOD detection.

  8.6  A 2026 THEORY RESULT CONVERGES WITH OUR RQ2 NEGATIVE. Section 4.6's
       0/20 calibration result is not contrarian: arXiv:2606.10777 (2026)
       shows standard reverse-KL EDL objectives yield non-vanishing epistemic
       uncertainty even in the infinite-data limit. Present RQ2 as convergent
       evidence, not a failed experiment.

  8.7  THE CLOSEST PRIOR WORK CONTRADICTS US, AND THAT IS THE CONTRIBUTION.
       Bayesian Evidential Learning for Few-Shot Classification
       (arXiv:2207.13137) is evidential + few-shot + Dirichlet, on
       ResNet-12/Conv-4, reporting miniImageNet 63.10/79.60 and CIFAR-FS
       73.96/86.92 (1-/5-shot) -- and it reports ECE IMPROVING (3.59% vs
       14.69%, mini 5-shot). It reports no OOD AUROC, no parameter counts,
       and META-TRAINS its backbone. Our RQ2 says evidential DEGRADES
       calibration. Both hold: BEL meta-trains a backbone and fuses two
       networks' evidence; we freeze the backbone and cap the trainable
       budget at <=31,744 params (one cell has 2). The regime boundary is
       the finding, and ours is the edge-relevant side of it -- meta-training
       a backbone is exactly what a 256 kB device cannot do. If a reviewer
       raises BEL, this is the answer; do not concede a contradiction.
