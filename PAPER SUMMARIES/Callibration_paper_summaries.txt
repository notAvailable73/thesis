==== PAPER: On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks ====

1. TITLE / AUTHORS / VENUE
   "On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks."
   Chuan Guo*, Geoff Pleiss*, Yu Sun*, Kilian Q. Weinberger (*equal contribution),
   Cornell University. ICML 2017 (Proc. 34th Int. Conf. on Machine Learning,
   Sydney; PMLR 70). arXiv:1706.04599v2 (3 Aug 2017).

2. CORE IDEA
   Modern deep nets (e.g. a 110-layer ResNet) are far more accurate than older
   nets (e.g. LeNet) but are badly *miscalibrated* — their confidence
   systematically exceeds their accuracy. The paper diagnoses why this happens
   and shows that a trivial post-hoc fix, temperature scaling (a single scalar),
   restores calibration better than more complex methods on most vision datasets.

3. METHOD / ARCHITECTURE
   Calibration is measured, not trained. Key metrics:
   - Reliability diagram: bin predictions by confidence, plot acc(B_m) vs
     conf(B_m); identity line = perfect calibration.
   - ECE = sum_m (|B_m|/n) * |acc(B_m) - conf(B_m)|  (weighted avg gap).
   - MCE = max_m |acc(B_m) - conf(B_m)|  (worst-case gap).
   Post-hoc calibrators (all fit on a held-out validation set, model frozen):
   histogram binning, isotonic regression, BBQ (Bayesian binning), Platt scaling,
   and the multiclass Platt variants — matrix scaling (W z + b), vector scaling
   (diagonal W), and TEMPERATURE SCALING. Temperature scaling:
       q_i = max_k  softmax(z_i / T)_k ,   T > 0 optimized by minimizing NLL on
   the validation logits. Because dividing logits by T does not move the argmax,
   accuracy is unchanged; T>1 softens/raises entropy, T->inf -> uniform (1/K).

4. KEY HYPERPARAMETERS / TRAINING DETAILS
   - ECE/MCE reported with M = 15 bins.
   - T fit against NLL on a validation set; solvable by 1-D convex optimization
     (conjugate gradient, ~10 iterations) or even line search.
   - Set T = 1 during training; find optimal T afterward on validation.
   - No new network weights; T is the only learned calibration parameter.

5. DATASETS & METRICS
   Vision: CUB Birds, Stanford Cars, ImageNet, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN, with
   ResNet, ResNet-Stochastic-Depth, Wide ResNet, DenseNet, LeNet.
   NLP: 20 News, Reuters (DAN-3), SST binary/fine (TreeLSTM).
   Metrics: ECE (primary), MCE, NLL, classification error.

6. IMPLEMENTATION NOTES
   - Root causes of miscalibration: increased depth/width (capacity), Batch
     Normalization, and reduced weight decay; also NLL overfitting after the
     LR drop (test error keeps dropping while NLL climbs).
   - Vector scaling collapses to ~constant entries, i.e. it recovers temperature
     scaling — "miscalibration is intrinsically low-dimensional."
   - Matrix scaling has O(K^2) params, overfits on many-class sets (Birds, Cars,
     CIFAR-100) and fails to converge on ImageNet. Avoid for large K.
   - Binning methods can change the argmax and hurt accuracy; temperature scaling
     never does. Requires a validation set drawn from the same distribution.

7. OFFICIAL CODE REPO
   http://github.com/gpleiss/temperature_scaling  (stated in the paper).

8. RELEVANCE TO MY THESIS
   Supplies the ECE/MCE calibration metrics and the temperature-scaling baseline
   my B-PEFT evidential framework must beat (or complement) when demonstrating
   that evidential uncertainty yields better-calibrated few-shot CNN predictions.


==== PAPER: Simple and Scalable Predictive Uncertainty Estimation using Deep Ensembles ====

1. TITLE / AUTHORS / VENUE
   "Simple and Scalable Predictive Uncertainty Estimation using Deep Ensembles."
   Balaji Lakshminarayanan, Alexander Pritzel, Charles Blundell (DeepMind).
   NIPS 2017 (31st Conf. on Neural Information Processing Systems, Long Beach).
   arXiv:1612.01474v3 (4 Nov 2017).

2. CORE IDEA
   Bayesian NNs give good uncertainty but are hard to implement and slow. The
   paper proposes "deep ensembles" — a non-Bayesian yet probabilistic recipe that
   is simple, parallelizable, needs almost no tuning, and matches or beats
   approximate Bayesian methods (notably MC-dropout) on calibration and, crucially,
   on out-of-distribution (dataset-shift) robustness.

3. METHOD / ARCHITECTURE  (see Algorithm 1)
   Three ingredients:
   (a) Proper scoring rule as the loss. Classification: softmax cross-entropy
       (= log-likelihood) or Brier score, both proper scoring rules. Regression:
       the net outputs mean mu(x) AND variance sigma^2(x); minimize Gaussian NLL
           -log p = 0.5*log sigma^2(x) + (y-mu(x))^2 / (2 sigma^2(x)) + const.
       Positivity of variance enforced via softplus log(1+exp(.)) + a floor 1e-6.
   (b) Adversarial training (optional smoothing). FGSM perturbation
           x' = x + eps * sign( grad_x  loss(theta,x,y) )
       add loss on (x',y) to the objective to smooth predictions locally.
   (c) Ensemble of M nets, each randomly initialized and trained on the FULL
       dataset with random shuffling (NO bagging). Combine as a uniform mixture:
       classification -> average the softmax probabilities; regression -> mixture
       of Gaussians approximated by a single Gaussian with mixture mean/variance:
           mu*(x) = (1/M) sum_m mu_m(x)
           sigma*^2(x) = (1/M) sum_m (sigma_m^2 + mu_m^2) - mu*^2.

4. KEY HYPERPARAMETERS / TRAINING DETAILS
   - Recommended defaults: M = 5 networks; eps = 1% of the input range per
     dimension (i.e. 0.01 * range; e.g. 2.55 for [0,255]).
   - Optimizer Adam, fixed LR 0.1, batch size 100 (unless noted).
   - UCI regression: 1 hidden layer, 50 units (100 for Protein / Year MSD), ReLU,
     40 epochs. MNIST: MLP 3x200 units, ReLU, batch norm. MC-dropout baseline
     used dropout rate 0.1 (0.5 was worse).

5. DATASETS & METRICS
   Regression: 1-D toy (y = x^3 + N(0,3^2)); UCI suite (Boston, Concrete, Energy,
   Kin8nm, Naval, Power, Protein, Wine, Yacht, Year MSD).
   Classification: MNIST, SVHN (VGG-style convnet), ImageNet (Inception).
   OOD / shift: MNIST->NotMNIST, SVHN->CIFAR-10, ImageNet dogs vs non-dogs.
   Metrics: NLL, classification error/accuracy, Brier score, RMSE (regression),
   and predictive entropy / max-prob histograms for OOD; accuracy-vs-confidence.

6. IMPLEMENTATION NOTES
   - Do NOT bag: a bootstrap sample sees only ~63% unique points and hurts NN
     ensembles; random init + shuffle alone gives diversity.
   - For regression you MUST learn the variance: an ensemble of MSE-only nets
     (using empirical variance of predictions) is badly under-dispersed — its 80%
     interval covered only ~20% of points. Learning sigma^2 fixes calibration.
   - Adversarial training helps most at M=1; the benefit shrinks as M grows and
     it is "not strictly necessary."
   - Deep ensembles give markedly higher entropy on unknown classes than
     MC-dropout, which stays overconfident — the main OOD advantage.
   - Captures two uncertainty sources: data ambiguity (proper scoring rule) and
     model uncertainty (ensembling).

7. OFFICIAL CODE REPO
   None mentioned in the paper. (The text links only a Torch CIFAR blog post and
   the NotMNIST dataset page — neither is an official code release. Do not
   assume an author repo.)

8. RELEVANCE TO MY THESIS
   Deep ensembles are the canonical strong uncertainty/OOD baseline and a
   conceptual foil to my evidential-Dirichlet head — both target epistemic
   uncertainty, but evidential PEFT aims for single-forward-pass uncertainty
   without the M-fold parameter/latency cost that is prohibitive on edge CNNs.
