  - B-Lora.pdf uses Bayesian *gates* to search quantization bit-width + rank
    (NOT predictive uncertainty).

    or Laplace) to get predictive uncertainty & calibration.

    give you the PEFT-Bayesianization machinery; your novelty is swapping their
    Gaussian-weight-posterior view for an evidential-output-distribution view on
    CNNs. Keep that distinction sharp when writing related work.

==== PAPER: Bayesian-LoRA (B-LoRA) — Optimal Quantization + Rank via Bayesian Gates ====

1. TITLE / AUTHORS / VENUE
   "Bayesian-LoRA: LoRA based Parameter Efficient Fine-Tuning using Optimal
   Quantization levels and Rank Values through Differentiable Bayesian Gates."
   Cristian Meo*, Ksenia Sycheva*, Anirudh Goyal, Justin Dauwels (TU Delft; Mila).
   Workshop on Advancing Neural Network Training (WANT) @ ICML 2024. arXiv:2406.13046.

2. CORE IDEA
   Standard LoRA fixes one rank for every weight matrix and does no quantization.
   B-LoRA jointly and automatically learns, per LoRA block, (a) the optimal rank
   and (b) the optimal quantization bit-width, by placing Bayesian priors on
   discrete "gates." Result: ~70% fewer bit-operations (BOPs, an energy proxy)
   with GLUE accuracy on par with LoRA/DyLoRA/AdaLoRA, and no per-dataset tuning.
   WARNING: this is efficiency/compression, NOT calibration/uncertainty.

3. METHOD / ARCHITECTURE
   - Builds on "Bayesian Bits" (Van Baalen 2020). A quantized value is a residual
     sum over bit-widths {2,4,8,16,32}: xq = x2 + z4(e4 + z8(e8 + z16(e16 + z32*e32))),
     where each e is the residual error refining precision and z_i in {0,1} are
     gating variables. Turning a gate off forces all higher gates off.
   - Bayesian priors on gates: p(z_m | z_n=1) = Bernoulli(e^-lambda) between
     consecutive active levels; posterior q(z_m|z_n=1) = Bernoulli(sigmoid(phi)).
   - SVD-structured LoRA (as in AdaLoRA): W = W0 + B E A, with diagonal vector
     E in R^r. Rank gates g_i multiply the diagonal entries so ranks can be pruned:
     Ê_i = (prod_{j<=i} g_j) * e_i. Prior p(g_{n+1}|g_n=1)=Bern(e^-lambda), p(g_1)=1.
   - Training objective (Eq.15): LoRA log-likelihood MINUS a quantization
     regularizer MINUS a rank regularizer (both are expected #active gates),
     weighted by lambda_q and lambda_r. Straight-through estimator (STE) for rounding.
   - Pseudocode (Algorithm 1, per block): quantize W,A,E,B; compute rank gates
     g_i from sigmoid(xi_j); apply gates to diagonal E; return
     Q_out( W̄x + B̄ · Q_E( Ē · Q_A( Āx ))). Separate quantizer per matrix.

4. KEY HYPERPARAMETERS / TRAINING DETAILS
   Backbone DeBERTaV3-base. Optimizer AdamW; LR 5e-4; batch 8; warmup 0; linear
   scheduler; weight decay 0; max seq len 256; seeds {0,1,2}. LoRA r_q=r_k=r_v=8,
   alpha=16. Regularizer weights lambda_q = lambda_r = 1. Quantizer constants
   zeta1=-0.1, zeta2=1.1, inference threshold t=0.34. Applied ONLY to W_q,W_k,W_v
   (W_o, W_f1, W_f2 frozen). Trainable-param count: #params = 6·l·r·d
   (l layers, d hidden). Only # epochs is tuned per dataset (7–50); everything else
   is shared across GLUE.

5. DATASETS & METRICS
   GLUE: MNLI, SST-2, CoLA, QQP, QNLI, RTE, MRPC, STS-B. Metrics: task metric
   (acc / F1 / Matthews / Pearson-Spearman), trainable #params, and BOPs
   (multiplications × operand bit-widths — a hardware-agnostic energy proxy).

6. IMPLEMENTATION NOTES
   - Task-specific head stays at full 32-bit precision (quantizing it hurts a lot).
   - Learned pattern: value matrices W_v get higher rank AND higher precision than
     W_k/W_q; later layers get larger ranks; many LoRA blocks collapse to rank 1.
   - LoRA blocks themselves are pushed to low precision (2–4 bits); pre-trained
     weights need more bits when the fine-tuning dataset is small (<10k).
   - Evaluated ablations: full B-LoRA (q+ra) vs quantization-only B-LoRA (q).
   - Only validated on GLUE (classification) — no generation tasks.

7. OFFICIAL CODE REPO
   No dedicated B-LoRA repo is given. The paper says it is built on the public
   BayesianBits repo (github.com/Qualcomm-AI-research/BayesianBits) and the
   AdaLoRA repo (github.com/QingruZhang/AdaLoRA). State this as "no own repo."

8. RELEVANCE TO MY THESIS
   Directly informs the efficiency/Pareto axis (RQ4) and adaptive-rank question
   (RQ1): its Bayesian gates could pick per-conv-block rank/precision on the edge,
   but it gives no uncertainty output, so it complements rather than replaces your
   evidential head.


==== PAPER: Laplace-LoRA — Bayesian Low-Rank Adaptation for Large Language Models ====

1. TITLE / AUTHORS / VENUE
   "Bayesian Low-Rank Adaptation for Large Language Models."
   Adam X. Yang*, Maxime Robeyns*, Xi Wang, Laurence Aitchison
   (Univ. of Bristol; UMass Amherst). ICLR 2024. arXiv:2308.13111.
   (This is the paper your proposal calls "BayesLoRA" — its real name is Laplace-LoRA.)

2. CORE IDEA
   Fine-tuned LLMs are badly overconfident, especially on small datasets. Laplace-
   LoRA adds calibration by applying a POST-HOC Laplace approximation over ONLY the
   LoRA parameters of an already-fine-tuned model. Because it changes nothing about
   the training pipeline and reasons over ~6M LoRA params instead of 7B weights,
   it is cheap; it "dramatically" reduces ECE/NLL while keeping accuracy.

3. METHOD / ARCHITECTURE
   - Start from a normally MAP-fine-tuned LoRA checkpoint theta_MAP.
   - Laplace = 2nd-order Taylor expansion of the log-joint around theta_MAP =>
     Gaussian posterior N(theta_MAP, Sigma), Sigma = (Hessian + lambda·I)^-1.
     Isotropic Gaussian prior N(0, lambda^-1 I).
   - Hessian is approximated by the Fisher information / Generalized Gauss-Newton,
     with Kronecker-factored (KFAC) structure per linear layer:
     F_l = sum_n E[(a_{l-1} a_{l-1}^T) ⊗ (g_l g_l^T)].
   - The two LoRA factors A (r×d) and B (d×r) are treated as two separate linear
     layers. One KFAC factor is small (r×r), the other is d×d (~4096) — too big,
     so it is stored via a LOW-RANK approx of rank n_kfac (done incrementally so
     the full factor is never formed).
   - Prediction: linearized Laplace — push posterior through the network linearized
     at theta_MAP to get a Gaussian over logits, N(f_MAP, Lambda),
     Lambda = (grad f)^T Sigma (grad f); then Monte-Carlo sample logits, softmax,
     average = Bayesian model average.
   - Prior precision lambda tuned by the closed-form Laplace marginal likelihood on
     the TRAIN set (no validation set needed) — a key selling point for low-data.
   - Two variants: LA = Laplace over ALL LoRA weights (best); LLLA = last-layer only.

4. KEY HYPERPARAMETERS / TRAINING DETAILS
   Backbone LlaMA2-7B (also RoBERTa-base/large, Mistral-7B in appendices).
   LoRA r=8, alpha=16, dropout 0.1, weight decay 0, LR 5e-5, linear scheduler.
   Batch size 4 (Llama) / 32 (RoBERTa); max seq len 300 (Llama) / 256 (RoBERTa).
   Fine-tune 10,000 iterations, checkpoint every 1000. LoRA applied to queries,
   values, and the output layer. Loss = standard next-token / class likelihood
   (Laplace is post-hoc, so training loss is unchanged).

5. DATASETS & METRICS
   In-distribution: 6 common-sense reasoning tasks — WG-S, WG-M (Winogrande),
   ARC-C, ARC-E, OBQA, BoolQ (multiple-choice / True-False, all <10k train).
   OOD (from OBQA checkpoint): ARC-C/ARC-E (near) and MMLU CS/Eng/Law/Health (far).
   Metrics: Accuracy, ECE, NLL. Baselines: MAP, MC-Dropout (10), checkpoint
   ensemble (3), deep/LoRA ensemble (3), temperature scaling.

6. IMPLEMENTATION NOTES
   - Full LA >> last-layer LLLA; LLLA helps on only some datasets, LA helps on all.
   - Naive Monte-Carlo sampling of the logit Gaussian BEATS the probit approximation
     and the Laplace bridge (which ignore covariance) — just sample.
   - Benefit persists even under optimal early stopping (accuracy peak).
   - The d×d Kronecker factor is the memory bottleneck — the low-rank n_kfac trick
     (incremental, never materialize full factor) is what makes it feasible.
   - Post-hoc, so it can wrap ANY good set of fine-tuned weights (e.g. Mixout).

7. OFFICIAL CODE REPO
   github.com/adamxyang/laplace-lora  and  github.com/MaximeRobeyns/bayesian_lora
   (built on the `laplace` and ASDL libraries).

8. RELEVANCE TO MY THESIS
   The canonical "post-hoc Bayesian PEFT for calibration" baseline: you can port
   last-layer/KFAC Laplace onto CNN adapters as a non-evidential uncertainty
   comparator, and its "tune prior on train set, no val set" idea is gold for your
   few-shot (1–10 shot) regime where a validation split is a luxury.


==== PAPER: BH-PEFT — Bayesian Hybrid Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning ====

1. TITLE / AUTHORS / VENUE
   "A Bayesian Hybrid Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning Method for Large Language
   Models." Yidong Chai, Yang Liu, Yonghang Zhou (Hefei Univ. of Technology);
   Jiaheng Xie (Univ. of Delaware); Daniel Dajun Zeng (Chinese Academy of Sciences).
   Business-analytics / IS venue (year not explicitly printed on the extracted
   pages; references cite works up to 2025, so treat as ~2024–2025).

2. CORE IDEA
   Hybrid PEFT (combining Adapter + LoRA + Prefix-tuning, as in He 2022's MAM/UniPELT)
   gives the best accuracy but (1) uses point estimates => no uncertainty and (2)
   forgets old data when re-tuned. BH-PEFT makes the hybrid PEFT modules Bayesian
   (variational Gaussian weights) to quantify uncertainty, and adds a "Bayesian
   dynamic fine-tuning" scheme where last round's posterior becomes next round's
   prior — mitigating catastrophic forgetting on streaming data.

3. METHOD / ARCHITECTURE
   - Two Bayesian PEFT modules on a Transformer block:
     (a) Bayesian Prefix-tuning on attention: prefix vectors P_k,P_v generated by a
         prefix encoder (down W_d^P in R^{d×r_P}, tanh, up W_u^P in R^{r_P×d});
         P_k,P_v concatenated to keys/values. W_d^P, W_u^P are Gaussian random vars.
     (b) Bayesian scaled parallel adapter on the feedforward layer:
         x_out = LayerNorm(x_rc + x_ffn + s · W_u^A f(W_d^A x_rc)), ReLU f, scale s;
         W_d^A in R^{d×r_A}, W_u^A in R^{r_A×d} are Gaussian random vars.
   - All four projection matrices get fully-factorized (mean-field) Gaussian
     posteriors q(W)=N(mu, diag(sigma^2)); original weights kept as delta/fixed.
   - Train by maximizing the ELBO = E_q[log p(D|W)] − sum KL(q(W)||p(W)).
     Prior p(W)=N(0, 0.1^2 I). First term via Monte-Carlo + reparameterization
     trick W = mu + sigma ⊙ eps, eps~N(0,I); log-likelihood is neg-cross-entropy
     (classification) or neg-MSE (regression). KL has closed form for the two
     Gaussians. Optimizer Adam; std parameterized as sigma_ij = g_ij^2.
     Init mu ~ U(-sqrt(6/d), sqrt(6/d)), g ~ U(delta/sqrt2, delta).
   - Uncertainty = variance of the (sampled) output distribution.
   - Bayesian dynamic FT: at round t, prior p_t(W) <- posterior q_{t-1}(W); re-run
     VI on the new dataset. KL-to-prior acts as principled regularization vs forgetting.

4. KEY HYPERPARAMETERS / TRAINING DETAILS
   Backbone RoBERTa-base (HF Transformers). Bottleneck r_A = 8, r_P = 8; adapter
   scaling s = 4. Prior mean 0, prior std 0.1 (per Blundell 2015). Mean-field VI +
   reparameterization; S Monte-Carlo samples (count not given in extracted text).
   FLAGGED MISSING: exact learning rate, #epochs, batch size, S, and delta live in
   Appendix E, which did not extract into the readable text — get these from the
   repo / appendix before reimplementing. Baselines' bottleneck sizes were tuned so
   total trainable params are comparable.

5. DATASETS & METRICS
   Five business tasks: Drug Reviews (regression, satisfaction 1–10), IMDB (binary
   sentiment), AG's News (4-class), SST2 (sentiment), CSQA (commonsense MCQ).
   Metrics: Acc/Precision/Recall/F1 for classification; MSE/MAE for regression;
   mean±std over 5 runs. Uncertainty evaluated via a REJECTION CURVE (reject most-
   uncertain predictions => accuracy should rise; ~+3% after rejecting top-10%).
   Dynamic-FT experiment starts from 10 samples on SST-2 and grows the set.
   Baselines: Adapter, LoRA, Prefix, APT, COMPACTER, RandLoRA (single-aspect);
   MAM Adapter, UniPELT (hybrid).

6. IMPLEMENTATION NOTES
   - Asymmetric roles: prefix-tuning for attention, scaled-parallel-adapter for FFN
     (mirrors He 2022's MAM design, just Bayesianized).
   - The g^2 std-parameterization + specific init are stated to accelerate ELBO
     convergence (follows Wang 2025) — don't use raw sigma.
   - Negative correlation between quantified uncertainty and correctness is the
     paper's core validation that the variance is meaningful.
   - Dynamic scheme's advantage is the KL-to-previous-posterior term, NOT mere
     weight re-initialization.

7. OFFICIAL CODE REPO
   github.com/s22s2s/BH-PEFT (stated in the paper).

8. RELEVANCE TO MY THESIS
   Closest architectural match to your "Bayesian PEFT" umbrella — a hybrid
   Adapter/LoRA/Prefix module made Bayesian by VI, with adapter serial/parallel
   placement decisions (RQ1) and uncertainty via output variance. Your thesis
   differs by (i) targeting CNNs not Transformers and (ii) using evidential/Dirichlet
   uncertainty instead of Gaussian-weight VI; its posterior-as-prior dynamic scheme
   is a nice extension idea for few-shot continual adaptation.


==== PAPER: BLoB — Bayesian Low-Rank Adaptation by Backpropagation ====

1. TITLE / AUTHORS / VENUE
   "BLoB: Bayesian Low-Rank Adaptation by Backpropagation for Large Language Models."
   Yibin Wang*, Haizhou Shi*, Ligong Han, Dimitris Metaxas, Hao Wang
   (Rutgers; MIT-IBM Watson AI Lab). NeurIPS 2024. arXiv:2406.11675.

2. CORE IDEA
   Post-hoc Bayesianization (e.g. Laplace-LoRA) splits posterior estimation into two
   stages and is capped by the point-estimate weights it inherits. BLoB instead
   learns the LoRA posterior's MEAN and COVARIANCE JOINTLY, in a single fine-tuning
   run, via Bayes-by-Backprop. Sampling during training even improves the mean.
   Better accuracy AND calibration (ECE/NLL) on in-dist and OOD than MAP/MCD/ENS/BBB/Laplace.

3. METHOD / ARCHITECTURE
   - ASYMMETRIC Bayesianization (key trick): in LoRA (W0 + B A), Bayesianize ONLY A
     with a diagonal Gaussian q(A)=N(M, Omega^2); keep B deterministic (Omega_B=0,
     B init 0). This equals a Gaussian posterior over the FULL weight with a
     singular/low-rank covariance Sigma_q = [I⊗B] diag(vec(Omega)^2) [I⊗B^T]
     (Theorem 3.1) — captures weight correlations, ~50% less memory than
     Bayesianizing both A and B, and avoids huge early-training sampling noise.
   - Low-rank Gaussian PRIOR centered at W0; Theorem 3.2 shows the full-weight KL
     reduces to KL over the small matrix A alone:
     KL = (1/2 sigma_p^2)(||M||^2 + ||Omega||^2) − sum log Omega_ij. Efficient.
   - Std parameterization Omega_ij = G_ij^2 (NOT softplus) — gives large gradients
     near zero-variance init => faster KL convergence (their analysis, App A.2).
   - LoRA-Flipout: apply Flipout to A only, so each example in a mini-batch gets
     independent weight noise (Eq.12) — big sample-efficiency win; lets K=1 sample
     per step in training.
   - KL RE-WEIGHTING schedule lambda_i = 2^i / (2^M − 1) over M mini-batches:
     fit data early, tighten complexity/KL late. Plus a "pseudo-rescaling" of dataset
     size (coeff gamma) so KL warm-up is consistent across datasets of different sizes.
   - Inference: draw N samples of W, average softmax probs. N=10 default (N=0 = use
     mean only).
   - Full training loop = Algorithm 1.

4. KEY HYPERPARAMETERS / TRAINING DETAILS
   Backbone LlaMA2-7B (also RoBERTa-base). LoRA r=8 on queries, values, output layer
   (follows Laplace-LoRA). BLoB-specific (Table 3/4): prior std sigma_p = 0.2;
   init hyperparam epsilon = 0.05 (G ~ U(eps/sqrt2, eps), M ~ U(-sqrt(6/n), sqrt(6/n)));
   pseudo-rescale gamma = 8; KL optimized by SGD with LR 0.01 (Llama) / 0.002 (RoBERTa).
   Training K = 1 sample/step; 5000 gradient steps (or 5 epochs; max LR raised
   1e-4->2e-4 in the 5-epoch setting). Baseline MAP uses weight decay 1e-5; MCD
   dropout p=0.1 (ensemble 10); ENS = 3 LoRAs. Follows PEFT-library defaults otherwise.

5. DATASETS & METRICS
   In-dist: WG-S, WG-M, ARC-C, ARC-E, OBQA, BoolQ (six common-sense reasoning).
   OOD from OBQA checkpoint: ARC-C/ARC-E (small shift), MMLU college Chemistry &
   Physics (large shift). Metrics: ACC, ECE, NLL. Baselines: MLE, MAP, MCD, BBB,
   ENS, Laplace-LoRA (LAP). Shared hyperparameters across datasets; no extra val set.

6. IMPLEMENTATION NOTES
   - Do NOT naively combine BBB + LoRA — it diverges early; the asymmetric-A design,
     the G^2 std param, and Flipout are ALL needed for fast, stable convergence.
   - N (inference samples) trades accuracy vs calibration: N=0 highest ACC, larger N
     lowers ECE/NLL — pick per your accuracy-vs-uncertainty priority.
   - Laplace-LoRA can outright fail under a shared (non-per-dataset) hyperparameter
     setting (e.g. ARC-C) — a fairness caveat BLoB highlights.
   - Only A is Bayesian, so the extra memory over plain LoRA is small.

7. OFFICIAL CODE REPO
   github.com/Wang-ML-Lab/bayesian-peft  (shared repo with TFB below).

8. RELEVANCE TO MY THESIS
   The strongest "train-time Bayesian LoRA" template: asymmetric Bayesianization +
   Flipout + KL-reweighting port cleanly to LoRA on 1x1/3x3 conv kernels in your CNN
   backbones, and its ECE/NLL/OOD (MMLU) protocol is a ready-made calibration &
   near/far-OOD evaluation blueprint. Your evidential-Dirichlet head is the intended
   contrast to its Gaussian-weight posterior.


==== PAPER: TFB — Training-Free Bayesianization for Low-Rank Adapters ====

1. TITLE / AUTHORS / VENUE
   "Training-Free Bayesianization for Low-Rank Adapters of Large Language Models."
   Haizhou Shi*, Yibin Wang*, Ligong Han, Huan Zhang, Hao Wang
   (Rutgers; UIUC; Red Hat AI). NeurIPS 2025. arXiv:2412.05723.
   (Same group as BLoB; TFB is its training-free successor.)

2. CORE IDEA
   Existing Bayesian LoRA needs training (BLoB) or post-training Hessian work
   (Laplace-LoRA). TFB turns ANY already-trained LoRA into a Bayesian one with NO
   gradient steps: it searches for the largest weight-posterior variance (within a
   low-rank isotropic Gaussian family) that keeps performance within a tolerance
   eps. This search is proven equivalent to (generalized) variational inference.
   Simple, cheap, and beats prior Bayesian-LoRA methods on uncertainty.

3. METHOD / ARCHITECTURE
   - Restrict the full-weight posterior to a LOW-RANK ISOTROPIC Gaussian:
     q(vec(W)) = N(mu_q, proj(sigma_q^2 I)) — one scalar sigma_q controls all noise.
   - Practical construction from a trained LoRA {B, A}:
       1. Compact SVD:  B = U diag(d) V^T.
       2. Regroup:  B' = U diag(d),  A' = V^T A  (so B'A' = BA, unchanged).
       3. Set mean M = A', and standard deviations Omega_ij = sigma_q / d_i
          (i.e. Omega = [sigma_q/d, ..., sigma_q/d]) — projecting isotropic full-space
          noise onto the low-rank space. Only A' is randomized (asymmetric, like BLoB).
     => storage for std drops from O(r n) to O(r); one shared sigma_q across layers.
   - Choosing sigma_q = VARIANCE MAXIMIZATION (Eq.8): on an in-distribution "anchor"
     dataset D with metric l and tolerance eps,
         max sigma_q  s.t.  |l(D | Bayesianized) − l(D | original)| <= eps.
     Solved by BINARY SEARCH over [sigma_q_min, sigma_q_max] (Algorithm 1); an
     8-point parallel grid-search + interpolation variant is given in App F.10.
   - Metric l: NLL for supervised anchor data (theoretically the right choice);
     accuracy/F1 also work; for unsupervised data, pseudo-label with the pre-Bayes
     model or use expected embedding norm. Anchor data can be the original training
     subset (no separate calibration set needed).
   - Theory (Thm 4.1/4.2): the construction yields Sigma_q = sigma_q^2 (I_n ⊗ [I_r;0]);
     under local convexity of NLL and prior std sigma_p > eps0, variance maximization
     == generalized VI (min lD + lambda·KL). With lambda=1/|D| it reduces to standard VI.
     TFB never has to specify sigma_p explicitly.
   - Inference: average N=10 posterior samples (BLoB protocol).

4. KEY HYPERPARAMETERS / TRAINING DETAILS
   No training of adapter weights. Tolerance: fixed RELATIVE change eps/p0 = 0.3%
   for NLL, 1% for accuracy (adaptive eps can do better). Single shared sigma_q found
   by binary search (explicit numeric [sigma_q_min, sigma_q_max] range not printed in
   main text). N = 10 test-time samples. LoRA rank follows BLoB (r=8). Backbones:
   Meta-Llama-3.1-8B (primary), plus Llama-2-7B, Meta-Llama-3-8B, Mistral-7B-v0.3.
   TFB is applied on top of various trained means: MLE-LoRA, MAP-LoRA, BLoB-mean.

5. DATASETS & METRICS
   In-dist: WG-S, WG-M, ARC-C, ARC-E, OBQA, BoolQ. OOD from OBQA: ARC-C/E (small
   shift), MMLU college Chemistry & Physics (large shift). Metrics: ACC, ECE, NLL,
   N=10 samples for all sampling methods. Baselines: MC-Dropout, Ensemble,
   Laplace-LoRA (LAP), MonteCLoRA, BLoB. Fine-tuning done for 5 epochs.

6. IMPLEMENTATION NOTES
   - The SVD regrouping (B'=U diag(d)) is essential: it makes noise on A' isotropic
     in the full-weight space; naive Omega breaks the theory.
   - Binary search only needs near-optimal sigma_q (strict monotonicity not required)
     — a few forward passes on the anchor set; very cheap in time & memory.
   - Overfitted checkpoints tolerate larger eps (more injectable variance).
   - Because it wraps a fixed mean, TFB improvements are additive on MLE/MAP/BLoB
     means (shaded/BLUE cells in their Table 1) — good as a plug-in calibrator.
   - Implementation deliberately matches BLoB's codebase for compatibility.

7. OFFICIAL CODE REPO
   github.com/Wang-ML-Lab/bayesian-peft  (same repo as BLoB).

8. RELEVANCE TO MY THESIS
   Highest edge-AI value: after your few-shot CNN adapter/LoRA is trained, TFB could
   inject calibrated uncertainty with ZERO extra training and O(r) memory — ideal for
   the latency/Pareto goal (RQ4) and 1–10-shot regime — giving you a training-free
   Gaussian-posterior baseline to compare against your evidential-Dirichlet approach.

CROSS-PAPER TAKEAWAYS FOR THE THESIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Lineage: Laplace-LoRA (post-hoc, ICLR'24) -> BLoB (in-training, NeurIPS'24) ->
  TFB (training-free, NeurIPS'25). BLoB & TFB share the github.com/Wang-ML-Lab/
  bayesian-peft codebase — the most reusable starting point for your CNN port.
* Shared eval recipe you can copy: ACC + ECE + NLL; in-dist common-sense tasks;
  OOD = fine-tune on one dataset, test on near (ARC) + far (MMLU) shifts. Your
  thesis already mirrors this with AUROC / FPR@95 + CIFAR-10-C corruptions.
* "Asymmetric Bayesianize A, keep B deterministic" (BLoB/TFB) is the practical trick
  that makes LoRA-on-conv-kernels stable — worth adopting even with an evidential head.
* GAP your thesis fills: all five model a GAUSSIAN posterior over WEIGHTS (VI or
  Laplace) on TRANSFORMER/LLM backbones. None uses EVIDENTIAL/DIRICHLET output
  uncertainty, none targets lightweight CNNs, and none operates in the true few-shot
  (1–10 shot) vision regime — that intersection is your contribution.
* Terminology caution for related work: cite B-Lora.pdf (Meo et al.) as
  quantization/rank compression, NOT as an uncertainty method; cite "BayesLoRA" in
  your proposal as Laplace-LoRA (Yang et al., ICLR 2024) — that is the paper meant.
