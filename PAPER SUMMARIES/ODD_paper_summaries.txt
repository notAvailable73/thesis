==== PAPER: A Baseline for Detecting Misclassified and Out-of-Distribution Examples in Neural Networks  [source: Baseline.pdf] ====

1. TITLE / AUTHORS / VENUE
   "A Baseline for Detecting Misclassified and Out-of-Distribution Examples in
   Neural Networks." Dan Hendrycks (UC Berkeley), Kevin Gimpel (TTI-Chicago).
   Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2017 (arXiv:1610.02136v3). Commonly
   called the "MSP baseline."

2. CORE IDEA
   Deep classifiers fail silently, giving high softmax confidence on wrong or
   out-of-distribution (OOD) inputs. The paper shows that, despite this, the
   *maximum softmax probability* (MSP) of correctly-classified in-distribution
   inputs is statistically higher than that of misclassified/OOD inputs, so MSP
   is a surprisingly effective, no-cost detector. It also standardizes the
   evaluation tasks/metrics for this problem, and offers one method that beats
   the baseline.

3. METHOD / ARCHITECTURE
   - Score: for input x, s(x) = max_k softmax(f(x))_k. Threshold the score:
     inputs with low MSP are flagged as error/OOD. (KL divergence of the softmax
     from uniform was also tried and gave near-identical AUROC/AUPR to MSP.)
   - "Abnormality module" (the method that beats MSP): attach an auxiliary
     decoder that reconstructs the input to a normally-trained classifier; freeze
     the classifier+decoder, then train a small red-layer "abnormality" branch on
     clean (normal) vs. noised/blurred/rotated (abnormal) examples with a sigmoid
     output that scores how abnormal x is. It uses the reconstruction residual
     (subtract + elementwise-square) plus the softmax vector as features.

4. KEY HYPERPARAMETERS / TRAINING DETAILS
   - MNIST classifier: 3-layer, 256-wide fully-connected, GELU nonlinearity,
     Adam, 30 epochs.
   - CIFAR-10/100: 40-4 Wide ResNet, 50 epochs, SGD with warm restarts (SGDR),
     GELU, standard mirroring + cropping augmentation.
   - No special loss beyond standard training; the detector is post-hoc.

5. DATASETS & METRICS
   - ID: MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 (plus NLP: IMDB, 20 Newsgroups, Reuters;
     speech: TIMIT). OOD: SUN scenes, Gaussian/Uniform noise, Omniglot, notMNIST,
     CIFAR-10bw, etc.
   - Metrics introduced here and reused everywhere after: AUROC, AUPR (reported
     separately with "In"/success and "Out"/error as the positive class), plus
     a random-detector "Base" rate.

6. IMPLEMENTATION NOTES
   - Key demonstration: mean softmax prob of *wrong* CIFAR-100 predictions is
     still ~66%, and random Gaussian noise into an MNIST net gives ~91% "confidence"
     — so MSP as an absolute confidence is misleading, but its *relative*
     separation still enables detection.
   - For CTC speech, they compute softmax while ignoring the blank-symbol logit.
   - Guidance for future work: always report the base classifier's accuracy
     alongside AUROC/AUPR, and test on multiple modalities/architectures.

7. OFFICIAL CODE REPO
   Stated in the paper: github.com/hendrycks/error-detection

8. RELEVANCE TO THESIS
   This is the softmax-overconfidence problem and the MSP OOD score that your
   evidential/Dirichlet uncertainty head is meant to outperform — MSP is the
   mandatory first baseline for the thesis' AUROC / FPR@95 OOD experiments.


==== PAPER: Enhancing the Reliability of Out-of-distribution Image Detection in Neural Networks (ODIN)  [source: Enhance.pdf] ====

1. TITLE / AUTHORS / VENUE
   "Enhancing the Reliability of Out-of-distribution Image Detection in Neural
   Networks." Shiyu Liang (UIUC), Yixuan Li (UW-Madison), R. Srikant (UIUC).
   ICLR 2018 (arXiv:1706.02690v5). Method name: ODIN.

2. CORE IDEA
   A purely post-hoc OOD detector for a pre-trained network (no retraining). Two
   cheap tricks — temperature scaling of the softmax and a small adversarial-style
   input perturbation — widen the softmax-score gap between ID and OOD, greatly
   improving on the MSP baseline (e.g., FPR@95 on DenseNet CIFAR-10 vs
   TinyImageNet(crop) drops from 34.7% to 4.3%).

3. METHOD / ARCHITECTURE
   - Temperature-scaled softmax: S_i(x;T) = exp(f_i(x)/T) / Σ_j exp(f_j(x)/T).
     Score = max_i S_i(x;T). Large T flattens logits and separates ID/OOD.
   - Input preprocessing (perturb to *increase* the top score, opposite of an
     adversarial attack):
         x_tilde = x - ε · sign( -∇_x log S_yhat(x;T) )
     Perturbation has a stronger effect on ID than OOD inputs.
   - Detector: feed x_tilde back through the net, compute S(x_tilde;T), compare to
     threshold δ (δ chosen so ID TPR = 95%).
   - Intuition (Taylor expansion): S ∝ (U1 − U2/2T)/T where U1 = mean logit gap of
     the top class vs the rest, U2 = spread of the remaining logits. Large T makes
     the score dominated by U1 (larger for ID); the perturbation exploits that ID
     images have a larger ‖∇_x log S‖.

4. KEY HYPERPARAMETERS / TRAINING DETAILS
   - Backbones: DenseNet-BC (L=100, growth k=12, dropout 0) and Wide ResNet
     WRN-28-10 (dropout 0), trained with SGD + Nesterov momentum 0.9.
     DenseNet: 300 epochs, batch 64; WRN: 200 epochs, batch 128. LR starts 0.1,
     ÷10 at 50% and 75% of training.
   - Detector hyperparameters: T searched over {1,2,5,10,20,50,100,200,500,1000}
     (they use T=1000 for all reported runs); ε over 21 evenly spaced values in
     [0, 0.004] (ε=0.0014 for CIFAR-10 models, 0.002 for CIFAR-100). Tuned to
     minimize FPR@95 on a *separate* OOD validation set (iSUN).

5. DATASETS & METRICS
   - ID: CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100. OOD: TinyImageNet (crop/resize), LSUN (crop/resize),
     iSUN, Gaussian noise, Uniform noise.
   - Metrics: FPR@95%TPR, Detection Error, AUROC, AUPR-In, AUPR-Out.

6. IMPLEMENTATION NOTES
   - Perturbation requires one extra forward+backward pass through the input
     (gradient of log-softmax w.r.t. x) — a real inference-latency cost, relevant
     if you benchmark edge latency.
   - Hyperparameters transfer well across OOD validation sets (low sensitivity).
   - Detection gets harder as ID/OOD get closer (they quantify this with MMD):
     resized crops (global structure) are near-OOD and harder than random crops.

7. OFFICIAL CODE REPO
   Stated in the paper: github.com/facebookresearch/odin

8. RELEVANCE TO THESIS
   ODIN is the canonical post-hoc temperature+perturbation OOD baseline; its
   gradient-based input cost is a concrete point of comparison for your "latency
   vs. uncertainty quality" Pareto analysis on edge hardware.


==== PAPER: A Simple Unified Framework for Detecting Out-of-Distribution Samples and Adversarial Attacks (Mahalanobis)  [source: Simple Unified.pdf] ====

1. TITLE / AUTHORS / VENUE
   "A Simple Unified Framework for Detecting Out-of-Distribution Samples and
   Adversarial Attacks." Kimin Lee (KAIST), Kibok Lee (U. Michigan), Honglak Lee
   (Google Brain / U. Michigan), Jinwoo Shin (KAIST/AItrics). NeurIPS (NIPS) 2018
   (arXiv:1807.03888v2). Method name: Mahalanobis / MDS.

2. CORE IDEA
   Instead of scoring in the "label-overfitted" softmax output space, score in the
   feature space. Fit class-conditional Gaussians with a shared (tied) covariance
   to a pre-trained network's features (Gaussian Discriminant Analysis, which is
   equivalent to the softmax classifier), and use the Mahalanobis distance to the
   nearest class centroid as the confidence score. One score handles BOTH OOD and
   adversarial inputs, and is unusually robust in small-data / noisy-label regimes.

3. METHOD / ARCHITECTURE
   - Fit per class c: empirical mean μ_c = (1/N_c) Σ f(x_i), and tied covariance
     Σ = (1/N) Σ_c Σ_{i:y_i=c} (f(x_i)−μ_c)(f(x_i)−μ_c)^T.
   - Confidence score: M(x) = max_c −(f(x)−μ_c)^T Σ^{-1} (f(x)−μ_c)  (log of the
     class-conditional Gaussian density at the closest class).
   - Two calibration techniques:
       (a) Input pre-processing: x_hat = x + ε·sign(∇_x M(x)) — perturb to
           increase the Mahalanobis score (analogous to ODIN but on M, not softmax).
       (b) Feature ensemble: compute M_ℓ(x) at the end of every dense/residual
           block (spatial dims average-pooled: F×H×W → F×1), then combine as a
           weighted sum Σ_ℓ α_ℓ M_ℓ(x); the weights α_ℓ are learned by a logistic-
           regression detector on validation data (ineffective layers get ~0 weight).
   - Class-incremental use: accommodate a new class by just computing its mean and
     updating the tied covariance — no backbone retraining.

4. KEY HYPERPARAMETERS / TRAINING DETAILS
   - Backbones: DenseNet-100 and ResNet-34; hidden feature dim 512 (ResNet).
   - The score itself is post-hoc (no retraining of the classifier). Only the
     logistic-regression layer weights + ε are tuned, via nested cross-validation
     on a validation set (1,000 images per ID/OOD pair).
   - Notably, hyperparameters can be tuned using ONLY in-distribution samples +
     FGSM adversarial samples (no real OOD data needed) and still beat ODIN.

5. DATASETS & METRICS
   - ID: CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN. OOD: TinyImageNet, LSUN, SVHN/CIFAR-10.
     Adversarial: FGSM, BIM, DeepFool, CW. Also class-incremental on CIFAR-100 /
     downsampled ImageNet.
   - Metrics: TNR@95%TPR, AUROC, Detection accuracy, AUPR.
   - Example: on ResNet CIFAR-100 vs LSUN, TNR@95 improves 45.6% (ODIN) → 90.9%.

6. IMPLEMENTATION NOTES
   - Low-level features often calibrate better than final features (see their
     per-block AUROC curves) — the feature ensemble is what closes the gap; using
     only the penultimate layer is materially weaker on some OOD sets.
   - Strongly robust when the training set is small or has random labels, where
     MSP and ODIN collapse — this is the key property for a few-shot setting.
   - A detector tuned on the *simple* FGSM attack generalizes to detect BIM,
     DeepFool, CW.

7. OFFICIAL CODE REPO
   Stated in the paper: github.com/pokaxpoka/deep_Mahalanobis_detector

8. RELEVANCE TO THESIS
   The most directly relevant classic baseline for your few-shot regime: a
   feature-space, class-conditional-Gaussian score that stays reliable with few
   or noisy samples, and could be combined with (or contrasted against) your
   evidential Dirichlet vacuity score for near-OOD detection.


==== PAPER: Energy-based Out-of-distribution Detection  [source: Energy Based.pdf] ====

1. TITLE / AUTHORS / VENUE
   "Energy-based Out-of-distribution Detection." Weitang Liu (UC San Diego),
   Xiaoyun Wang (UC Davis), John D. Owens (UC Davis), Yixuan Li (UW-Madison).
   NeurIPS 2020 (arXiv:2010.03759v4). Method name: Energy score / EBO.

2. CORE IDEA
   Replace the softmax confidence score with an energy score derived from the
   logits. The energy is theoretically aligned with the input density p(x) (low
   energy ↔ high likelihood), whereas softmax confidence is a *shifted* biased
   version of it and thus poor for OOD. Energy works both as a parameter-free
   post-hoc score AND as a trainable objective to sharpen the ID/OOD energy gap.

3. METHOD / ARCHITECTURE
   - Free-energy score over logits f(x): E(x;f) = −T · log Σ_i exp(f_i(x)/T).
     Use the negative energy −E(x;f) as the ID-ness score (higher → ID); threshold
     it. Computed via a single logsumexp over logits; parameter-free at T=1.
   - Connection to softmax: log max_y p(y|x) = −E(x;f) + f_max(x) at T=1, i.e.
     the softmax score equals the energy shifted by the max logit — the shift is
     what biases softmax and hurts OOD separation.
   - Optional energy-bounded fine-tuning (needs auxiliary OOD data):
         min_θ  E_{Dtrain_in}[−log F_y(x)]  +  λ · L_energy
         L_energy = E_{in}[ (max(0, E(x_in) − m_in))^2 ]
                  + E_{out}[ (max(0, m_out − E(x_out)))^2 ]
     i.e. cross-entropy + two squared-hinge penalties that push ID energy below
     m_in and OOD energy above m_out (a two-margin loss; a single-margin variant
     was less stable).

4. KEY HYPERPARAMETERS / TRAINING DETAILS
   - Backbone: WideResNet. Fine-tuning: λ = 0.1, 10 epochs, initial LR 0.001 with
     cosine decay, batch 128 (ID) / 256 (auxiliary OOD).
   - Margins tuned on a validation set: m_in ∈ {−3,−5,−7}, m_out ∈ {−15,−19,−23,−27}.
     Optimal reported: m_in = −23 (CIFAR-10) / −27 (CIFAR-100); m_out = −5 (both).
   - Temperature: T=1 is best; larger T monotonically worsens FPR95 (opposite of
     ODIN's finding, because here energy is used directly, not the softmax).
   - Auxiliary outlier data = 80 Million Tiny Images (with CIFAR overlaps removed).

5. DATASETS & METRICS
   - ID: CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN. OOD (six): Textures, SVHN, Places365,
     LSUN-Crop, LSUN-Resize, iSUN.
   - Metrics: FPR@95, AUROC, AUPR.
   - Headline: post-hoc energy cuts average FPR95 by 18.03% vs MSP on CIFAR-10;
     energy fine-tuning beats Outlier Exposure by ~5.2% (CIFAR-10) and ~10.55%
     (CIFAR-100) FPR95, while keeping ID accuracy roughly unchanged.

6. IMPLEMENTATION NOTES
   - Post-hoc energy needs no extra data, no perturbation, no retraining — just
     logsumexp on existing logits; cheapest strong baseline to add.
   - Energy histograms are smooth (softmax histograms are spiky at ~1.0), which is
     why energy separates ID/OOD better.
   - Two separate margins matter: the paper found E(x_in)−E(x_out) can be stable
     while the absolute values drift, so tune m_in and m_out independently.

7. OFFICIAL CODE REPO
   Stated in the paper: github.com/wetliu/energy_ood

8. RELEVANCE TO THESIS
   The energy score is the strongest cheap logit-based OOD baseline to compare
   against your evidential Dirichlet-strength (S) uncertainty; note the conceptual
   parallel — evidential total evidence S and negative energy both measure
   "amount of support" and both avoid softmax's overconfidence.


==== PAPER: OpenOOD: Benchmarking Generalized Out-of-Distribution Detection  [source: Open ODD.pdf] ====

1. TITLE / AUTHORS / VENUE
   "OpenOOD: Benchmarking Generalized Out-of-Distribution Detection." Jingkang
   Yang, Pengyun Wang, Dejian Zou, ... Dan Hendrycks, Yixuan Li, Ziwei Liu (NTU
   S-Lab et al.). NeurIPS 2022, Datasets & Benchmarks Track (arXiv:2210.07242v1).
   This is OpenOOD v1.

2. CORE IDEA
   The field lacked a unified, fair benchmark, so methods cherry-picked datasets.
   OpenOOD is a single well-structured codebase that re-implements 35 methods
   spanning four neighboring fields — anomaly detection (AD), open-set recognition
   (OSR), OOD detection, and model-uncertainty/data-augmentation — and evaluates
   them under one "generalized OOD detection" framework across 9 benchmarks with
   careful data cleaning and a near-OOD / far-OOD split.

3. METHOD / ARCHITECTURE (of the benchmark)
   - Taxonomy of methods: classification-based (post-hoc + training-time),
     density-based, distance-based, reconstruction-based.
   - Post-hoc scores included: MSP, ODIN, MDS (Mahalanobis), EBO (energy), GRAM,
     DICE, GradNorm, ReAct, MLS (max-logit), KL-Matching, VIM, KNN.
   - Training-time: ConfBranch, G-ODIN, CSI, MOS, VOS, LogitNorm; with outlier
     data: OE, MCD, UDG. Uncertainty/aug: MC-Dropout, DeepEnsemble, TempScaling,
     Mixup, CutMix, PixMix.
   - Near-OOD = semantic shift only; Far-OOD = additional covariate/domain shift.
     Benchmarks carefully remove ID-overlapping images from OOD sets (e.g. 1,207
     TinyImageNet images removed for CIFAR-10).

4. KEY HYPERPARAMETERS / TRAINING DETAILS
   - Unified backbones: LeNet (MNIST-ID benchmarks), ResNet-18 (CIFAR & TIN-ID),
     ResNet-50 (ImageNet-ID).
   - Training methods use a common recipe: SGD, LR 0.1, momentum 0.9, weight decay
     0.0005, 100 epochs (to avoid over-tuning).
   - Post-hoc hyperparameters: try only the 5 most common values, pick by AUROC on
     a validation set (ID val + an OOD val set, e.g. OpenImage-O for ImageNet).

5. DATASETS & METRICS
   - 9 benchmarks: 1 AD (MVTec-AD), 4 OSR (MNIST-6/4, CIFAR-6/4, CIFAR-50/50,
     TinyImageNet-20/180), 4 OOD (ID = MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet-1K,
     each with near- & far-OOD sets — e.g. ImageNet near-OOD Species/ImageNet-O/
     OpenImage-O, far-OOD Texture/MNIST/SVHN).
   - Metrics: FPR@95, AUROC (main), AUPR (reported as "FPR@95 / AUROC / AUPR").

6. IMPLEMENTATION NOTES / KEY FINDINGS
   - Data-augmentation methods (esp. PixMix, also CutMix/Mixup/Ensemble) were the
     single most effective category (PixMix hit 93.1% near-OOD AUROC on CIFAR-10).
   - Extra outlier data "seems not necessary" — its advantage was narrow.
   - Post-hoc methods make strong progress and generally are no worse than
     training-based methods; MDS is good on MNIST but weak on CIFAR, whereas KNN
     stays strong from MNIST to ImageNet.
   - CIFAR benchmarks are NOT easier than ImageNet ones. OSR benchmarks align with
     near-OOD. Pitfall noted: each method was run only once (no seed averaging).

7. OFFICIAL CODE REPO
   Stated in the paper: github.com/Jingkang50/OpenOOD

8. RELEVANCE TO THESIS
   This is the evaluation harness and near-/far-OOD protocol your thesis should
   adopt for fair AUROC/FPR@95 comparison, and it already implements MSP/ODIN/
   Mahalanobis/Energy so you can plug your evidential PEFT model in beside them.


==== PAPER: OpenOOD v1.5: Enhanced Benchmark for Out-of-Distribution Detection  [source: OpenOdd V1.5.pdf] ====

1. TITLE / AUTHORS / VENUE
   "OpenOOD v1.5: Enhanced Benchmark for Out-of-Distribution Detection." Jingyang
   Zhang, Jingkang Yang, Pengyun Wang, ... Yixuan Li, Ziwei Liu, Yiran Chen, Hai Li
   (Duke, NTU S-Lab, UW-Madison, et al.). Journal of Data-centric Machine Learning
   Research (JDMLR) 2024 (arXiv:2306.09301v5).

2. CORE IDEA
   A stricter, larger-scale successor to OpenOOD v1. It fixes evaluation pitfalls
   (confusing terminology, inconsistent datasets, test-set leakage in
   hyperparameter tuning), scales to ImageNet-1K and foundation models (CLIP,
   DINOv2), and adds "full-spectrum" OOD detection that must simultaneously handle
   semantic shift (detect) and covariate shift (still classify correctly).

3. METHOD / ARCHITECTURE (of the benchmark)
   - Formal definitions: (Def 1) D_OOD is OOD w.r.t. D_ID if every region with
     significant OOD probability mass has arbitrarily small ID mass; (Def 2) the
     objective is to minimize open-space risk R_O(f).
   - Detector form: G(x;f) = 1[S(x;f) ≥ λ] where S is any OOD score and λ a
     threshold. OOD is the positive class, ID the negative.
   - Full-spectrum: add covariate-shifted ID (csID) images to the ID test set;
     the detector must keep csID as ID while flagging semantic OOD.
   - Rigor: strictly disjoint OOD train / val / test category sets
     (Y_train ∩ Y_test = ∅); hyperparameters tuned on held-out ID+OOD *validation*
     data (never test), unlike ODIN/MDS/G-ODIN originals.
   - ~40 methods supported (5 more post-hoc, 4 more training, 5 more augmentations
     than v1), grouped: post-hoc inference / training w/o outliers / training w/
     outliers / data augmentation.

4. KEY HYPERPARAMETERS / TRAINING DETAILS
   - CIFAR-10/100 & ImageNet-200: ResNet-18, 100 epochs, SGD momentum 0.9,
     LR 0.1 with cosine annealing, weight decay 0.0005, batch 128 (CIFAR) / 256
     (ImageNet-200). 3 independent training runs (v1 used 1).
   - ImageNet-1K: post-hoc evaluated on torchvision pre-trained ResNet-50, ViT,
     Swin; training methods on ResNet-50 (fine-tune 30 epochs, LR 0.001, batch 256,
     wd 0.0005). Foundation models share a ViT-Base backbone (CLIP zero-shot with
     prompt ensemble; DINOv2 via linear probe).
   - Post-hoc hyperparameter search is automated using ID+OOD validation, pick by
     AUROC.

5. DATASETS & METRICS
   - 4 standard benchmarks: CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet-200, ImageNet-1K.
     CIFAR near-OOD = CIFAR-100/CIFAR-10 + TIN; far-OOD = MNIST/SVHN/Textures/
     Places365. ImageNet near-OOD = SSB-hard + NINCO; far-OOD = iNaturalist/
     Textures/OpenImage-O. OOD train sets: TIN-597 (CIFAR), ImageNet-800 (IN-200).
   - Full-spectrum csID sets: ImageNet-C (corruptions), ImageNet-R (style),
     ImageNet-V2 (resampling). Metrics: AUROC (main), AUPR, FPR@95, plus ID Acc.

6. IMPLEMENTATION NOTES / KEY FINDINGS
   - No single winner across benchmarks; per-dataset ranking varies a lot. Strong
     post-hoc scores in their tables include KNN, VIM, RMDS, ASH, ReAct (and MDS
     is weak on CIFAR/ImageNet but the RMDS "relative Mahalanobis" fix helps a lot,
     esp. near-OOD). RotPred and G-ODIN lead among training-w/o-outlier methods.
   - "Data augmentation + strong post-processor" is the most effective combo (e.g.
     AugMix + SHE is best for full-spectrum near-OOD on ImageNet-1K). Augmentations
     help less clearly on full-spectrum far-OOD.
   - Full-spectrum detection is hard for ALL current methods (covariate-shifted ID
     gets wrongly flagged as OOD).
   - DINOv2 linear-probe beats a supervised ImageNet model on every OOD case and
     on ID accuracy; zero-shot CLIP mainly helps far-OOD; most existing detectors
     aren't yet compatible with CLIP, so its potential is likely under-tapped.
   - Limitation: all methods considered are discriminative (assume a classifier).

7. OFFICIAL CODE REPO
   Stated in the paper: github.com/Jingkang50/OpenOOD
   Leaderboard: zjysteven.github.io/OpenOOD

8. RELEVANCE TO THESIS
   This is the current gold-standard, leakage-free evaluation protocol to report
   your B-PEFT results under: its ResNet-18 recipe and near/far + full-spectrum
   (covariate-shift, cf. your CIFAR-10-C robustness goal) splits map almost
   one-to-one onto your planned OOD/calibration experiments.
