==== PAPER: A Closer Look at Few-shot Classification ====

1. TITLE / AUTHORS / VENUE
   "A Closer Look at Few-shot Classification." Wei-Yu Chen (CMU), Yen-Cheng Liu
   & Zsolt Kira (Georgia Tech), Yu-Chiang Frank Wang (National Taiwan Univ.),
   Jia-Bin Huang (Virginia Tech). Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2019
   (arXiv:1904.04232v2, dated 12 Jan 2020).

2. CORE IDEA
   A fair-comparison empirical study of few-shot classifiers under a single
   consistent codebase. It shows (a) deeper backbones shrink the accuracy gap
   between sophisticated methods, (b) a simple "Baseline++" (distance/cosine
   classifier) is competitive with meta-learning SOTA, and (c) under domain
   shift a plain fine-tuning Baseline beats meta-learners. The message: much of
   the reported "progress" was an artifact of inconsistent evaluation.

3. METHOD / ARCHITECTURE
   Baseline: train feature extractor f_theta + linear classifier C(.|W_b) on
   many base classes with cross-entropy; then FREEZE f_theta and train a fresh
   linear classifier C(.|W_n) on the few novel-class support examples.
   Baseline++: identical, but the classifier scores each class by cosine
   similarity between feature and a learned per-class weight vector (prototype),
   s_ij = f(x_i)^T w_j / (||f(x_i)|| ||w_j||), then softmax. This explicitly
   reduces intra-class variation. The paper also re-implements MatchingNet,
   ProtoNet, RelationNet (metric-based) and MAML (init-based) in the same
   testbed via episodic meta-training M(.|S) conditioned on a support set S.

4. KEY HYPERPARAMETERS / TRAINING DETAILS
   Optimizer Adam, initial LR 1e-3, cross-entropy loss, all trained from
   scratch. Baseline/Baseline++: 400 epochs, batch 16; novel-class fine-tuning
   = 100 iterations, batch 4. Meta-learners: 60,000 episodes (1-shot) /
   40,000 (5-shot); 5-way, 16 query/class. Aug: random crop, L-R flip, color
   jitter. Baseline++ multiplies cosine by a learnable per-class scalar;
   RelationNet swaps L2 for softmax; MAML uses first-order approximation.
   Backbones: Conv-4 (84x84) up to ResNet-10/18/34 (224x224).

5. DATASETS & METRICS
   mini-ImageNet (64/16/20 split), CUB-200-2011 (100/50/50), the new
   cross-domain mini-ImageNet->CUB, plus Omniglot and Omniglot->EMNIST in the
   appendix. Metric: 5-way 1-/5-shot Top-1 accuracy, mean over 600 test
   episodes with 95% confidence intervals.

6. IMPLEMENTATION NOTES
   Data augmentation is critical — prior Baseline results were underestimated
   because augmentation was omitted (overfitting). Reducing intra-class
   variation matters with shallow backbones but less with deep ones (measured
   via Davies-Bouldin index). Under domain shift, freeze-and-refit Baseline
   wins; adding "further adaptation" (fix features, train a new softmax) rescues
   MatchingNet/MAML but can hurt ProtoNet when domain gap is small.

7. OFFICIAL CODE REPO
   https://github.com/wyharveychen/CloserLookFewShot (stated in the paper).

8. RELEVANCE TO MY THESIS
   Its Baseline++ cosine/prototype classifier and freeze-backbone-then-refit
   protocol are the natural non-PEFT reference point and evaluation harness for
   my frozen-CNN few-shot adaptation experiments.


==== PAPER: Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning (MAML) ====

1. TITLE / AUTHORS / VENUE
   "Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning for Fast Adaptation of Deep Networks."
   Chelsea Finn, Pieter Abbeel, Sergey Levine (UC Berkeley; Abbeel also OpenAI).
   ICML 2017, PMLR vol. 70 (arXiv:1703.03400v3).

2. CORE IDEA
   Learn a weight initialization theta such that a NEW task can be solved with
   only a few gradient steps on a handful of examples. It is model-agnostic
   (any gradient-trained model) and task-agnostic (classification, regression,
   RL), adding NO extra meta-parameters — it simply optimizes for
   "easy-to-fine-tune" parameters.

3. METHOD / ARCHITECTURE
   Bi-level optimization. Inner loop (task adaptation), one step:
       theta'_i = theta - alpha * grad_theta L_Ti(f_theta)
   Outer loop (meta-update) across sampled tasks T_i ~ p(T):
       theta <- theta - beta * grad_theta  SUM_i L_Ti(f_theta'_i)
   The meta-gradient differentiates THROUGH the inner update, so it needs
   second-order terms (Hessian-vector products). Pseudocode: sample task batch;
   for each task compute adapted theta'_i from K examples; evaluate the adapted
   model's loss on fresh query points; sum and backprop into theta. A first-
   order variant simply drops the second derivative.

4. KEY HYPERPARAMETERS / TRAINING DETAILS
   Inner step alpha, meta step beta. Losses: cross-entropy (classification),
   MSE (regression). Meta-optimizer Adam (supervised), TRPO (RL). Classifier
   backbone: 4 modules of 3x3 conv, 64 filters (32 for MiniImagenet to curb
   overfitting), batchnorm, ReLU, max/strided pooling, softmax.
   Omniglot 5-way: alpha=0.4, 1 inner step (train) / 3 (eval), meta-batch 32;
   20-way: alpha=0.1, 5 steps. MiniImagenet: 5 inner steps alpha=0.01 (train),
   10 (eval), meta-batch 4 (1-shot) / 2 (5-shot), 15 query/class, 60,000 iters.

5. DATASETS & METRICS
   Omniglot and MiniImagenet (here split 64 train / 12 val / 24 test) for
   classification; a sinusoid-regression toy task; RL (2D navigation,
   MuJoCo half-cheetah & ant). Metric: 5-/20-way 1-/5-shot accuracy, 95% CI.

6. IMPLEMENTATION NOTES
   The first-order approximation reaches nearly identical accuracy (ReLU nets
   are locally almost linear, so second derivatives ~ 0) while giving ~33%
   speedup — practically important. The meta-learned model keeps improving with
   MORE gradient steps at test time than it was trained for. Uses automatic
   differentiation through the inner update; batchnorm throughout.

7. OFFICIAL CODE REPO
   github.com/cbfinn/maml (supervised/regression) and github.com/cbfinn/maml_rl
   (RL), both stated in the paper.

8. RELEVANCE TO MY THESIS
   MAML is the gradient-based-adaptation counterpoint to my frozen-backbone
   PEFT approach; its inner/outer episodic loop and first-order trick are useful
   as a comparison baseline and as a template for how much few-shot adaptation
   can be squeezed from a small parameter update.


==== PAPER: Matching Networks for One Shot Learning ====

1. TITLE / AUTHORS / VENUE
   "Matching Networks for One Shot Learning." Oriol Vinyals, Charles Blundell,
   Timothy Lillicrap, Koray Kavukcuoglu, Daan Wierstra (Google DeepMind).
   NIPS 2016 (arXiv:1606.04080v2, 29 Dec 2017).

2. CORE IDEA
   A non-parametric one-shot classifier that maps a small labelled support set
   plus an unlabelled query directly to a label via an attention mechanism over
   learned embeddings — no fine-tuning needed for new classes. Its second
   contribution is the training principle "test and train conditions must
   match", i.e. episodic training that mimics the few-shot test task.

3. METHOD / ARCHITECTURE
   Prediction is a support-label-weighted sum:
       y_hat = SUM_i a(x_hat, x_i) * y_i
   with attention a = softmax over cosine similarity c(f(x_hat), g(x_i)) of two
   embedding networks f (query) and g (support). This subsumes kernel density
   estimation and k-NN. Full Context Embeddings (FCE): g becomes a bidirectional
   LSTM over the whole support set, and f becomes an attLSTM performing K
   read-attention steps over g(S) — so each embedding is conditioned on the
   entire episode instead of being computed in isolation. Objective (episodic):
       maximize  E_{L~T} E_{S,B~L} SUM_{(x,y) in B} log P_theta(y | x, S).

4. KEY HYPERPARAMETERS / TRAINING DETAILS
   Cosine-similarity attention + softmax. Embedding CNN = 4 modules of 3x3 conv,
   64 filters, batchnorm, ReLU, 2x2 max-pool; Omniglot images 28x28 (final
   feature 1x1x64), miniImageNet 84x84. For full ImageNet, f/g are initialized
   from a pretrained Inception classifier and then trained on 5-way 1-shot
   episodes. FCE adds an LSTM with K processing/unrolling steps.

5. DATASETS & METRICS
   Omniglot; miniImageNet — INTRODUCED in this paper (100 classes, 600
   images each, 84x84, 80 train / 20 test); full ImageNet with two held-out
   splits (L_rand and the harder, fine-grained L_dogs); and a one-shot Penn
   Treebank language task. Metric: N-way k-shot accuracy (5-/20-way, 1-/5-shot).

6. IMPLEMENTATION NOTES
   Episodic training is the core trick — "one-shot learning is easier if you
   train for one-shot." FCE helps ~2% on miniImageNet but is negligible on
   Omniglot. Optional support-set fine-tuning overfits massively but can still
   add a little accuracy on heavily regularized nets. The model degrades on the
   fine-grained L_dogs split because training samples classes uniformly rather
   than from fine-grained groups. Cost grows with support-set size (non-
   parametric memory).

7. OFFICIAL CODE REPO
   None mentioned in the paper. (Only auxiliary data-split URLs are given, e.g.
   the miniImageNet image list at https://goo.gl/e3orz6 — not source code.)
   Do not assume an official implementation from this PDF.

8. RELEVANCE TO MY THESIS
   Establishes the episodic N-way K-shot protocol and the cosine-attention /
   embedding-comparison paradigm I evaluate against; miniImageNet (defined here)
   is one of my in-distribution benchmarks.


==== PAPER: Prototypical Networks for Few-shot Learning ====

1. TITLE / AUTHORS / VENUE
   "Prototypical Networks for Few-shot Learning." Jake Snell (Univ. of Toronto),
   Kevin Swersky (Twitter), Richard S. Zemel (Univ. of Toronto / Vector
   Institute). NIPS 2017 (arXiv:1703.05175v2, 19 Jun 2017).

2. CORE IDEA
   Represent each class by a single "prototype" = the mean of its support
   examples in a learned embedding space, and classify a query by a softmax over
   its (negative) distances to the prototypes. A deliberately simple inductive
   bias that beats more complex meta-learners in the low-data regime; it also
   extends cleanly to zero-shot learning.

3. METHOD / ARCHITECTURE
   Prototype:  c_k = (1/|S_k|) * SUM_{x_i in S_k} f_phi(x_i).
   Query distribution:  p(y=k|x) = softmax_k( -d(f_phi(x), c_k) ).
   Train by minimizing -log p(true class) with SGD over episodes. Pseudocode
   (Algorithm 1): sample N_C classes; per class sample N_S support + N_Q query;
   compute prototypes from support; accumulate loss d(f(x),c_k) + log SUM_k'
   exp(-d(f(x),c_k')) over query points. KEY design choice: use SQUARED
   EUCLIDEAN distance, not cosine. Justification: squared Euclidean is a Bregman
   divergence, so the algorithm equals mixture-density estimation with an
   exponential-family cluster model, and the class mean is the optimal prototype;
   with Euclidean distance the model is provably a linear classifier in feature
   space (w_k = 2 c_k, b_k = -c_k^T c_k). Zero-shot: c_k = g(v_k), an embedding
   of class meta-data.

4. KEY HYPERPARAMETERS / TRAINING DETAILS
   Loss = negative log-likelihood (softmax over distances). SGD with Adam.
   Omniglot: LR 1e-3, halved every 2000 episodes; embedding = 4 conv blocks
   (64 filters, 3x3, batchnorm, ReLU, 2x2 max-pool) -> 64-D (Omniglot) or
   1600-D (miniImageNet); shared encoder for support & query; only batchnorm as
   regularization. Train with HIGHER "way" than test (Omniglot 60-way;
   miniImageNet 30-way for 1-shot, 20-way for 5-shot) and MATCH train-shot to
   test-shot; 15 query/class (miniImageNet). CUB zero-shot: LR 1e-4, weight
   decay 1e-5, 50 classes, 10 query/class, unit-normalized prototypes.

5. DATASETS & METRICS
   Omniglot and miniImageNet (Ravi & Larochelle 64/16/20 splits) for few-shot;
   CUB-200-2011 for zero-shot (312-D attribute meta-data, GoogLeNet features).
   Metric: 5-/20-way 1-/5-shot accuracy over 600–1000 episodes, 95% CI;
   50-way 0-shot accuracy for CUB.

6. IMPLEMENTATION NOTES
   Euclidean distance >> cosine (cosine is not a Bregman divergence) — the
   single most impactful choice. Higher training "way" improves generalization
   by forcing finer decisions. One prototype per class suffices; learning a
   per-dimension variance gave no gains. For zero-shot, unit-normalize the
   meta-data prototypes since they live in a different domain from image
   features. In the 1-shot case Prototypical and Matching Networks coincide.

7. OFFICIAL CODE REPO
   None stated in the paper. (It only cites a third-party repo,
   github.com/reedscot/cvpr2016, as the source of pre-extracted CUB image
   features — not the ProtoNet implementation.)

8. RELEVANCE TO MY THESIS
   Prototype = class-mean-in-embedding is the metric backbone my evidential head
   sits on; its distance-based softmax and episodic training map directly onto
   my few-shot setup, and its "distance-to-class-representation" scores are a
   natural quantity to convert into Dirichlet evidence for calibrated,
   uncertainty-aware classification.
